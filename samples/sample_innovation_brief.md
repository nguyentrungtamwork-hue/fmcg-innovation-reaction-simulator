# Sample FMCG Innovation Brief — FreshPlus Herbal Cool

**Brand:** FreshPlus
**Product:** FreshPlus Herbal Cool — low-sugar herbal tea with natural cooling ingredients
**Category:** Ready-to-drink tea (RTD)
**Launch market:** Vietnam (urban, tier-1 cities first)

## Concept
A bottled herbal tea positioned as a daily "natural cooling refresh" for urban office workers and young adults, with significantly less sugar than mainstream bottled teas.

## Benefit
Helps the body feel refreshed and cool naturally, with less sugar guilt.

## Functional claims
- 50% less sugar than leading RTD teas
- Contains traditional cooling herbs (chrysanthemum, mulberry leaf)
- No artificial flavors

## Emotional claims
- "A calm reset in the middle of a hot day"
- Modern take on a familiar herbal ritual

## Packaging
- 450ml PET bottle, frosted-look label
- Soft green + cream palette
- Pack copy emphasizes "natural cooling" and "less sugar"

## Price
- Slightly premium (~15–20%) vs mainstream bottled teas
- Pack size: 450ml single bottle; 6-pack online

## Target consumers
- Urban office workers, 22–35
- Health-leaning but not extreme; sensitive to sugar
- Buy bottled drinks weekly from CVS and supermarkets
- Active on TikTok and Facebook

## Usage occasions
- Mid-afternoon at work
- After lunch in summer
- Quick refresh on commute

## Channels
- Convenience stores (primary)
- Supermarkets
- TikTok Shop (6-pack bundle)
- Selected e-commerce platforms

## Competitors
- Mainstream sweetened bottled teas
- Zero-sugar bottled teas
- Functional / herbal drinks
- Bottled water with flavor

## Media plan
- TikTok short-form creator content
- Facebook awareness posts
- 2 mid-tier KOL partnerships (lifestyle + wellness)
- In-store shelf wobblers

## Sampling plan
- 4-week sampling at office-district CVS chains
- Sampling booths near 5 large office buildings

## Promotion plan
- Launch month: buy 1 get 1 at convenience chains
- TikTok Shop: 6-pack bundle at ~12% off
- Loyalty stamp card at supermarket partner

## Known risks / assumptions
- Consumers may not believe the "natural cooling" claim without education
- Premium price may suppress trial in value-seeking segment
- Herbal taste may be perceived as too medicinal by mainstream consumers
- Heavy reliance on TikTok may miss older office workers
